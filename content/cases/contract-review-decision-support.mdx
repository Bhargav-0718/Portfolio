## The Brief

Contracts are long and dense. Novice readers struggle to interpret them, and
legal professionals need real time to read each one, clause by clause, even
though most clauses are routine and a few carry most of the risk.

Earlier work had treated the pieces separately: models that classify clauses,
and models that summarise documents. The aim here was one deployable workflow
that does all three jobs: classify every clause, rank it by how much it
matters, and summarise the contract, then hand a legal team a report they can
act on.

## The Hypotheses

The study tested three bets:

- **Domain pretraining should matter.** A model pretrained on legal text
  (LegalBERT) should classify contract clauses better than general-purpose
  BERT, RoBERTa, or keyword rules.
- **Long contracts need a different summariser.** A large-context LLM fed
  overlapping chunks and merged hierarchically should beat BART and PEGASUS,
  which struggle with input length and factual drift on long documents.
- **A label isn't a reading order.** Grouping the 47 clause types into a small
  number of criticality tiers, set by legal experts, should let reviewers
  read the riskiest clauses first.

## The Investigation

The pipeline runs in four stages: ingest and segment the document, classify
each clause, map clauses to tiers and generate analytics, then summarise.

**Data.** More than 13,000 expert-labelled clauses from 510 commercial
contracts in CUAD, across 47 categories. Preprocessing removed whitespace
artifacts, headers, footers and enumerations, detected clause boundaries from
linguistic and punctuation cues, and produced a combined training corpus with
a class-balanced 80/10/10 split.

**Classification.** LegalBERT with a dropout layer (p = 0.1) and a linear head
to 47 classes, fine-tuned for 3 epochs (batch size 8, learning rate 2×10⁻⁵,
10% linear warm-up, 512-token sequences, AdamW with 0.01 weight decay) on an
NVIDIA A100, about 22 minutes per epoch. Prediction is single-label (argmax)
for efficiency: a deliberate simplification, since CUAD is really multi-label.
BERT-base, RoBERTa-base and a keyword rule-based classifier were run on the
same split with the same settings as baselines.

**Summarisation.** GPT-4.1-mini, chosen over BART and PEGASUS for its larger
context window. Contracts were split into overlapping 2,500-character chunks
(200 characters of overlap) to keep context across clause boundaries, and the
chunk summaries merged into one 200–250 word summary covering obligations,
liabilities, parties and termination rights.

**Prioritisation.** Two contract practitioners and a legal academic each
assigned all 47 categories to five tiers, blind to each other (Fleiss
κ = 0.672, substantial agreement). The 17 disagreements were settled by
discussion.

**Product.** A Streamlit interface on top, with ReportLab, Matplotlib and
Seaborn producing a PDF report (tier distribution, clause analytics,
visualisations and the summary) and a CSV of every clause with its label and
tier. Stack: Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.8, Transformers 4.49, OpenAI SDK.

I did all of the implementation and technical work.

## The Finding

On the CUAD test set, LegalBERT reached **77.2% accuracy** and a **weighted F1
of 0.735** across 47 clause types (78.9% and 0.750 on validation). It matched
RoBERTa on accuracy and beat it on precision and weighted F1; the keyword
baseline managed 0.513 F1, which shows how poorly rules handle paraphrased
clauses.

GPT-4.1-mini summaries scored a **BERTScore F1 of 0.786**, ahead of BART and
PEGASUS on ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L and BERTScore.

In a user study, five legal professionals saved **67% of their review time** on
average compared with manual review, and rated the report 4.1/5 for clarity
and 4.0/5 for tier accuracy.

The paper was presented at ICCIS 2026 at BITS Pilani, K K Birla Goa Campus, in
September 2026.

## The Open Questions

- **Single-label is a simplification.** CUAD is multi-label; the next step is
  multi-label classification with sigmoid thresholds.
- **Coverage.** CUAD is English commercial contracts only. Employment,
  government and non-English documents are untested; MAUD and ACORD data, and
  cross-lingual LegalBERT variants, are the obvious next steps.
- **Upstream errors carry through.** Summaries depend on how well text is
  extracted, and the tier mapping reflects three annotators' judgement, which
  may not hold across jurisdictions or contract types.
- **The user study was small.** Five reviewers is a strong signal, not proof.
